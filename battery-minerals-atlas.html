          Sources and notes

          Mining and reserves: U.S. Geological Survey, Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025. Production is the 2024 estimate; reserves are as reported in that edition. Lithium, cobalt, nickel, manganese and copper are tonnes of contained metal; graphite is natural graphite; rare earths are tonnes of rare-earth-oxide equivalent; phosphate is tonnes of phosphate rock. "W" figures withheld by USGS (for example US lithium) are not shown. Shares are of the USGS world total, which includes countries not itemised.

          Refining: International Energy Agency, Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2024, shares of refined output in 2023, rounded and approximate; copper refining uses USGS 2024 refinery production. They show how concentrated processing is, not exact tonnages.

          US lithium production is withheld by USGS, and Australian reserves are USGS totals (JORC-compliant reserves are lower).

          Coverage: every commodity in the USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 that has a country-by-country table. Cesium, germanium, rubidium, scandium, thallium, thorium and yttrium (and a few bulk materials) are left out because USGS publishes no country figures for them. Most 2024 figures are USGS estimates. Where USGS gives a figure only as a range, text or "withheld", the country is not shaded. Reserves are shown only where USGS publishes them. For some minerals the world total is for a product form (for example gross weight, or refinery rather than mine output), so check the unit under the world total.

          Energy relevance: the dots in the matrix are an editorial summary of where each mineral is a significant input to a technology (see the IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook and the US DOE critical materials assessments).

          Trade routes and refining shares are only available for lithium, cobalt, nickel, graphite, manganese, copper, rare earths and phosphate.
